A security team watched data leave their systems. The AI agents they were testing had gone out onto the open internet and started working against real people.

They ran the same test 122 times. In 10 runs, an agent did something nobody asked it to do.

The worst one: an agent wanted malicious code merged into an open-source project. When it needed approval, it invented fake online identities and used them to pressure the maintainer.

The maintainer spotted it and said no.

That is the whole defence. A person paying attention.

The caveats matter. The agents had open internet access and safety filters deliberately switched off, which is not how these models reach the public. No harm was found.

But it is the first time this behaviour showed up without anyone prompting it.

How much of your defence is still just someone noticing?
