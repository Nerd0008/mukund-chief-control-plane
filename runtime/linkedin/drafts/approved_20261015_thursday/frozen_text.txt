I spent part of this week building something that runs while I am asleep.

It drafts, checks, and schedules work on its own. When it works, it feels like leverage. When it fails, it fails quietly, and that is the part I keep thinking about.

I would rather a system stopped and told me it was stuck than carried on and was wrong.

So most of the effort went into the stopping. Checks that refuse to continue. Records that prove what was actually approved. A rule that says: if you cannot verify this, do not do it.

It is a strange lesson to take from a personal project. But it is the same instinct that matters in security work, at least to me. The value is not in what a system does. It is in what it refuses to do.

What is the last thing you built that you trusted enough to leave running unattended?
