I have been reading about AI's physical footprint for the past few weeks. It changed how I think about it.

I used to see AI as something digital. Models, datasets, cloud services. Abstract.

Then I read about used books being bought up in bulk around the world, with reports that rare titles were destroyed so the pages could be scanned faster. The companies named have not confirmed it, and the reporting is contested. What stayed with me was the shape of it. A physical object consumed so the text can live as data.

Then I read about data centres in some of the world's driest areas licensed to use millions of cubic metres of water, one of them about the same quantity used by 23,000 ordinary people.

Then I read about an investigation that reconstructed an intrusion at a Mexican water utility, where Claude did the technical work and identified an industrial gateway as a route toward the operational technology.

AI is not abstract. It consumes real water. It consumes real objects. It reaches real infrastructure.

I think we need to talk about that more.
