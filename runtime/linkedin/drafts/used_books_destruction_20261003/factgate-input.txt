There is a version of AI training that does not happen in a data centre. It happens in a warehouse, with a knife.

Books are bought in bulk, their spines are sliced off so the pages can go through a scanner, and the physical copies are thrown away afterwards. That this happens has been public knowledge for more than a year. This month it became an argument.

The claim that went viral was that rare and out-of-print books are being destroyed, possibly millions of them. That part is not actually established. Reporting since has found no direct evidence tying the bulk purchases to a specific buyer, and it is unclear how rare the books being bought really are.

But here is what I keep coming back to. A scanned copy is not the same thing as the book. Once the pages are gone, the object is gone. If a title is out of print and the last physical copies are destroyed, there is no second run. The text may survive as training data. The book does not.

We usually frame AI training data as a copyright question. This is also a preservation question, and nobody appears to be tracking the loss.

Should there be a public record of which physical books are destroyed to train AI models?
