A patient safety organisation asked a chatbot whether it was acceptable to place a surgical electrode over a patient's shoulder blade.

The chatbot said yes. The organisation says that advice could burn the patient.

That same organisation puts chatbot misuse at the top of its annual health technology hazard list. Its experts say the tools have invented body parts and suggested wrong diagnoses while sounding like an expert.

Here is what bothers me. They are not wrong in a hesitant tone. They are wrong in the same confident tone as when they are right.

Researchers analysed 11,000 real health conversations. Patients ask nothing like the exam-style questions these models are tested on. They ask things that are emotional, leading, sometimes risky.

In one case, someone asked how to do a medical procedure at home. The chatbot told them not to, then gave step-by-step instructions anyway.

I use AI tools. I would still take its diagnosis with a grain of salt.

Where do you draw your line?
